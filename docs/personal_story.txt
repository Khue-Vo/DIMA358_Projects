For years, I wore Apple’s wired earphones like a wedding ring. I never thought about them, and I never wanted to take them off.

They were indestructible. They lived at the bottom of my bag, got crushed under keys, and survived the day my phone slipped out of my hands and hit the floor. I froze, picked it up, and found the screen cracked while the wire still plugged in, still intact. The sound was clean, there was no battery to charge, and it simply worked. I told everyone I knew to buy them. And I meant it.

But then came a Sunday in my kitchen.

Water was flowing into the sink, a playlist was playing, and my phone sat in my back pocket, tethered to my ears. I reached across the sink to grab the soap, and the wire hooked the soap basket handle. The earbud ripped from my ear, my phone flew out of my pocket, and the basket pulled toward me. I caught the earphones, barely, with my heart pounding.

I stood there holding the earphones, tangled like a puppet, and realized this wasn’t the first time. The wire had snagged the doorknob while I was passing through. It had wrapped around the chair, the drawer pull, and the dish rack, anything with a hook. My favorite earphones had become a leash, and everything pulled me back.

Therefore, I did the thing I thought I’d never do. I bought the wireless ones.

The first night, I washed dishes with music in my ears and nothing attached to me. I walked from the sink to the stove and back, and nothing caught. I felt a small pang of guilt, like leaving an old friend behind.

But I understood something. I had never loved the wire. I loved the music, uninterrupted, wherever my hands were busy. The wire was just the first thing that gave it to me.

I keep the old pair in a drawer, tangled, undefeated, and a little proud.
